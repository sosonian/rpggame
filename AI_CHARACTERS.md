# AI-Driven Characters: Design & Cost Analysis

> Companion to [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) (§6.2 Traits, §6.3 Relationships).
> Covers how characters are driven by rules and by AI language models (LLMs), and what that costs.
> Items marked **(proposal)** are suggestions waiting for a decision.
>
> Prices are Anthropic Claude API list prices as of November 2026. They change over time; re-check before
> deciding.

## 1. Decisions so far

| # | Decision |
|---|----------|
| 1 | **Every character runs on rules.** A chosen set of important characters is *also* played by AI. |
| 2 | The game has a **written plot with designed (story) characters**. The number of important characters is therefore limited. |
| 3 | Other characters are **generated randomly, following the rules**. Once the player **encounters** one, that character is played by AI too. |
| 4 | The game runs in **real time**. The basic time unit is **10 real-world minutes** (what game time it equals is TBD; see §7). |
| 5 | The first build is a **Chapter 1 demo**: one local county facing an enemy invasion. The number of AI-played characters is limited (see §8). |
| 6 | **Dialogue is choice-based.** The player chooses from a few options, based on the characters' traits and backgrounds. There is no free typing. |
| 7 | AI-played characters have **their own intents**, such as becoming famous, taking revenge on someone, or simply succeeding in life. These come from their **traits and background** (§5). *(2026-11-24)* |
| 8 | AI-played characters are **played like a human player**: they act **in real time**, pursuing their intents with the same kinds of actions as the player (§5). *(2026-11-24)* |
| 9 | **The plot is fully free.** There are no story locks: AI-played characters may change the plot, even kill or ruin story characters. The story must react to whatever happens (§5.8). *(2026-11-24)* |
| 10 | **AI-played characters may plot to kill the player, with no guaranteed warning** (§5.6). *(2026-11-24)* |
| 11 | **Full AI everywhere:** AI-played characters think just as often when the player is far away (§5.4). *(2026-11-24)* |
| 12 | **Intents are hidden until learned** by talking, observing, rumours, spying or skills (§5.7). *(2026-11-24)* |
| 13 | **Danger depends on the starting position.** The player can start in a more important position, such as the mayor's secretary or the lord's bodyguard, which naturally comes with rivals from the start. **A secret murder is very hard:** it needs skill, experience and courage, and a sensible character weighs the penalty of failure first (§5.6). *(2026-11-24)* |

**In short: rules everywhere, AI for the player's "circle", and that circle lives its own lives.**
This is the hybrid approach from the earlier analysis (kept in §10). Its cost is still practical (see §8–§9).

## 2. Character tiers

Every character belongs to one tier. All tiers share the same character data (§3) and the same rules engine.

| Tier | Who | Created by | Brain | How many |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Story characters** | Designed characters in the plot: your lord, mentor, rival, love interest, the enemy commander… | Written by hand: personality, backstory, secrets, intents | **AI** (always, once they enter the story) | Small and fixed per chapter |
| **2. Encountered characters** | Generated characters the player has met | Generated by the rules, then promoted to AI on first encounter | **AI** while relevant, otherwise rules | Capped (see §6) |
| **3. Background characters** | Everyone else in the world | Generated by the rules | **Rules only** | Any number |

### 2.1 What counts as an "encounter" (TBD, proposal)
A generated character is promoted to AI when any of these happens:
- the player has a **conversation** with them (opens a dialogue)
- they **join** the player's unit, band or household
- they become the player's **friend, rival, lover, mentor or family**
- they become **directly involved** in a plot event with the player, e.g. a duel or a betrayal

Merely seeing a name in a list or on the map does **not** count.

### 2.2 Promotion and demotion
- **Promotion:** the game writes a short **profile** of the character from their traits, background, memories and
  intents. The AI uses this profile from then on.
- **Demotion (proposal):** if a promoted character has had nothing to do with the player for a long time
  (e.g. 2–3 game years, or after the chapter ends), they go back to rules only. They keep their memories and
  intents, so if the player meets them again, they are promoted again and remember the past.
- Story characters are never demoted while their story is active.

## 3. Shared character data (the foundation)

The AI never replaces this data. It only reads it and suggests changes, which the rules check.

**Perception (owner's decision, 2026-11-27; details later):** characters also have **perceptions** of each
other: what they believe about another character's traits. A character may know only some of another's
traits, and may be wrong. See [EVENTS.md §7.3](EVENTS.md).

| Part | Content | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Traits | Personality, education, lifestyle, health, fame (PLAN §6.2) | Brave, Greedy, Schemer, Wounded |
| Skills | Diplomacy, Martial, Stewardship, Intrigue, Learning | Martial 12 |
| Background | Origin, profession, social rank, key past events | Miller's son, deserted the royal army |
| Opinions | What they think of each other character, with reasons | −35 toward the player: "Stole my promotion" |
| Relationships | Friend, rival, lover, mentor, family | Rival: Sir Osric |
| Memories | Short records of important things that happened to them | "Year 414: the player saved my life at Ashford" |
| **Intents** | Their ambition and current aims, with reasons and progress (§5.1) | Become famous; avenge their brother |
| **Plan** | AI-played characters only: the actions they are working on now (§5.3) | Buy a better sword → learn Brann's patrol route → challenge him |
| **Knowledge** | What they have seen, heard or been told; they act only on this (§5.2) | "Heard the bridge is guarded at night" |
| Needs | Food, tools, money ([ITEMS.md §7](ITEMS.md)) | Hungry; owes the innkeeper 5 silver |
| Secrets | Hidden facts (story characters especially) | Secretly in contact with the invaders |
| Stress (TBD) | Pressure from acting against their own nature | Kind character ordered to burn a village |

**Personality changes through events** in the same way for every tier:
- Events add or remove traits. Surviving a battle may turn Craven into Brave; being betrayed may cause Paranoid.
- Repeated behaviour builds toward a trait: many duels lead to Duelist, many lies to Deceitful.
- Memories feed opinions, relationships and **intents**. Each memory has a weight, and it fades over time.
- For AI-played characters, the AI may *suggest* a trait change ("this experience hardened him").
  The rules decide whether it happens.

## 4. What the AI does (and doesn't do)

### 4.1 Rules vs AI

| Task | Tier 3 (rules) | Tier 1–2 (AI-played) |
|---|---|---|
| **Choosing intents** (§5.1) | Rules, from traits and background | Rules propose; **AI may adjust and explain** |
| **Planning how to reach them** (§5.3) | Simple rule-based goals (e.g. "work, eat, save money") | **AI makes a plan** from legal actions |
| **Acting moment to moment** (walking, working, buying, fighting) | Rules | **Rules carry out the AI's plan** in real time |
| Opinion, trait and relationship maths | Rules | Rules (AI may suggest changes) |
| **Reacting in dialogue with the player** | Pre-written lines chosen by rules | **AI writes the reply and picks the reaction** |
| **Important decisions** (join a plot, betray, propose marriage, desert, attack) | Weighted random choice by rules | **AI chooses from the legal options and gives a reason** |
| Event text involving them | Template text | **AI personalises the text** (optional) |

### 4.2 The golden rule: the AI proposes, the game decides
1. The game sends:
   - the character's profile, memories, intents and knowledge
   - the situation
   - a list of **legal** options
2. The AI returns **structured data**:
   - the chosen option or plan
   - a short reason
   - its dialogue text (if any)
   - optional suggestions, such as an opinion change, a new intent, or a memory to record
3. The game **checks** the result and applies it with its own rules.
4. If the call fails, is slow, or is refused, the game uses the **rule-based choice**. The game always works
   without AI.

### 4.3 Choice-based dialogue (decision 6)

The player never types free text. A conversation works like this:

1. **The rules build the player's options, by chance.** The rules collect the options that make sense, then give
   each a **chance** from the character's personality and the situation. The **top 5** are shown.
   Full method and example in [ACTIONS.md §2.4](ACTIONS.md).
   - **Personality:** traits, skills and background, e.g. a Cruel or Bandit-born character is more likely to
     *Threaten*.
   - **Situation:** who the other person is to you, opinion, what is at stake, risk, mood, witnesses.
     For example, lying to someone vital to you has a low chance.
   - Some options need something specific: *Remind him of Ashford* needs the shared memory of that battle.
2. **The AI (optional) writes the options' wording** in the player character's voice. The intent of each option
   is fixed by the rules.
3. **The player picks an option.**
4. **The AI-played character responds.** The AI writes their reply in character and picks their reaction from a
   list of legal outcomes, such as agree, refuse, become angry or ask for more. These outcomes can be chosen
   by the same chance method (proposal). The rules then apply the effects.
   **Their intents colour the reply:** a character seeking fame jumps at a dangerous mission; one seeking
   revenge asks what you know about their enemy.
5. Repeat for 2–4 exchanges, then the conversation ends.
6. **AI-played characters can start conversations too:** they may walk up to the player to ask for help,
   offer a job, propose a deal, challenge or threaten them (§5.6).

Why this works well:
- The **rules stay in control** of what is possible.
- Calls are **short and cheap** (small outputs).
- There's **no risk of players typing their way around the game**.
- Every character still **sounds different**, because the AI writes from their personality, memories and intents.

## 5. Intents and real-time play (decisions 7 and 8)

AI-played characters are not just conversation partners waiting for the player. They are **players in their own
right**: each has their own intents and pursues them in real time, alongside the player, under the same rules.

### 5.1 Intents

**Every character has intents.** Tier 3 characters pursue them through simple rules; AI-played characters pursue
them through AI planning. Each character has:
- **one main ambition**, their long-term life goal, e.g. "become famous"
- **one to three current aims**, shorter goals, often steps toward the ambition or reactions to events,
  e.g. "get promoted to sergeant", "pay off my debt"

**Types of intent (proposal; the list will grow):**

| Intent | Example | Typical sources |
|---|---|---|
| Fame and glory | Become a famous warrior; win a tournament | Ambitious, Brave; a knight's younger son |
| Revenge | Avenge a brother; ruin the man who shamed me | A memory of a serious wrong; stronger if Cruel or Paranoid, weaker if Kind |
| Success and wealth | Become rich; own a shop; climb out of poverty | Greedy, Diligent; merchant or peasant background |
| Rank and power | Become captain, knight, lord | Ambitious; noble or soldier background |
| Love and family | Marry someone; protect one's children | Gregarious, Kind; has a family in town |
| Duty and loyalty | Serve the lord faithfully; defend the town | Honest, Content, Zealous; sworn oath |
| Survival and escape | Get out of the county before the invaders come | Craven, Paranoid; refugee background |
| Faith | Spread the faith; protect the shrine | Zealous; priest background |
| Knowledge | Learn to read; find an old ruin | Scholar, high Learning |
| Hidden agenda | Help the invaders; steal the treasury | Story characters (spies, traitors), Deceitful |

**Each intent records (proposal):**
- the **type** and the **target**, e.g. revenge on Captain Brann
- **strength** (0–100), meaning how much it matters
- the **reason**, linked to a trait, background or memory, e.g. "Brann left my brother to die at Ashford"
- **progress** and **status**: active, achieved, failed or abandoned
- **visibility**: secret, or known to certain characters (§5.7)

**How intents change:**
- **Created at start:** for story characters by the writer; for generated characters by the rules,
  from traits and background.
- **Created by events:** a new memory can create an intent. If your brother is killed, you want revenge.
  If someone saves your life, you want to repay them.
- **Achieved:** a new ambition grows from it, e.g. "become sergeant" leads to "become captain".
- **Fading or abandoned:** strength fades without reinforcement; conflicting traits weaken it,
  e.g. a Content character gives up ambition; failure can break it.
- **Changed by AI suggestion:** the AI may suggest a new or changed intent with a reason. The rules check it
  against the character's traits and memories before accepting it.

### 5.2 Played like a human player
An AI-played character follows **the same rules as the player**:
- **Same actions:** move around the map, talk, work a job, buy and sell, craft, train, give gifts, ask favours,
  spread rumours, steal, join or leave a unit, fight, flee. Their list of actions is the player's list.
- **Same limits:** they need food and money ([ITEMS.md §7](ITEMS.md)), they get tired and wounded, and they can
  die.
- **Same knowledge limits:** they act only on what they have **seen, heard or been told**. They do not know
  what the game knows. A rumour may be false; they can be deceived, just like the player.
- **No cheating:** no free gold, no knowing the player's secrets unless they found out.

### 5.3 How real-time AI play works: three layers
AI calls take seconds and cost money, so the AI cannot decide every move in real time. Instead it works like a
person who **decides, then acts**:

| Layer | Question | Who | How often | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ambition** | *Why?* | Rules propose, AI may adjust | Rarely, after big events | "Avenge my brother, killed through Captain Brann's cowardice" |
| **Plan** | *What?* | **AI** | When the plan is done, fails, or something important happens; also a regular review | 1. Buy a better sword. 2. Find out Brann's patrol route. 3. Challenge him at the harvest festival. |
| **Action** | *How?* | **Rules** | Continuously, in real time | Walk to the smithy, haggle, buy the sword, walk to the tavern… |

- **A plan, also called an idea, is a short list of actions** (proposal: 2–5). Plans are not chosen from a fixed
  list: an **event sparks them and the AI writes them** (see the false tax officer example in
  [IDEAS.md §2](IDEAS.md)). Each entry is an **action**: something the game
  already knows how to do, with the details filled in. The actions are the same ones the player can do.
  The player picks them with the mouse and menus; the AI picks them by writing a plan.
  The full list is in [ACTIONS.md](ACTIONS.md). How ideas work is in [IDEAS.md](IDEAS.md).
- **The rules carry out each action** in real time: pathfinding, work, trading, fighting. This costs nothing.
- **When an action cannot be done** (the shop is closed, the target left town), the rules try an obvious fix.
  If that fails, the AI is asked to re-plan.

**Example: a plan written by the AI**

> Marta, a widowed innkeeper (Greedy, Diligent). Ambition: *become rich*. Current aim: *earn 20 silver before
> winter*. She hears that soldiers are arriving because of the invasion. The AI writes this plan:

| # | Action + details | What the rules do in real time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `buy` 10 barrels of beer from the brewer, at most 4 silver | Walk Marta to the brewery, haggle using her skills, move the beer and coins |
| 2 | `hire` 1 helper from the town, at most 1 silver a week | Find a willing townsperson, agree the wage |
| 3 | `talk_to` the captain, topic: *sell beer to the garrison* | Start a conversation (an AI call if the captain is AI-played) |
| 4 | `work` at the inn until the soldiers arrive | Serve customers each day, earning money by the rules |
| 5 | `sell` beer at a higher price when the soldiers come | Sell at the market price, which the invasion has raised |

If the captain refuses at action 3, that action fails, and the AI is asked again. It might change the plan to
*sell beer at the market instead*.

**Which actions exist** is designed in [ACTIONS.md](ACTIONS.md).

The game checks every action: a character can't `poison` without poison, or `attack` someone they can't reach.

### 5.4 When the AI thinks: triggers (proposal)
An AI-played character's AI is called when:
- its **plan is finished** or has **failed**
- something **important happens to it or near it**: it is attacked, insulted or helped; it witnesses a crime;
  it hears important news; its target appears
- it **meets the player** or another AI-played character with something to discuss
- an **important decision** is pending (betray? desert? accept the offer?)
- the **regular review** comes round: once per time unit (10 real minutes) at most, if nothing else triggered it

**Full AI everywhere (decision 11):** every AI-played character thinks on every trigger, at most about once per
real minute, **wherever it is**. Distance from the player does not matter.
- The player learns about distant events as **news or rumours**, or by seeing the results
  ("while you were away, Edric became sergeant").
- Only characters **inside the cap** (§6) are AI-played. Characters demoted to rules (§2.2) run on rules
  wherever they are.
- **Queue priority:** when many characters need to think at once, those near the player go first, and distant
  ones may wait a few seconds. They still get their turn.

### 5.5 AI characters dealing with each other
- AI-played characters can **meet, talk, trade, ally, compete and fight** with each other, not only with the player.
- **One call per exchange (proposal):** when two AI-played characters interact, a single AI call plays both
  sides and returns the outcome and a short summary. The rules check and apply it.
- The player may hear about it later as **news or rumour**, or see it happen if nearby.

### 5.6 AI characters and the player
- AI-played characters **seek the player out** when the player is useful to their intents: to ask for help,
  offer work, recruit them into a plot, flirt, or demand repayment.
- They can **compete** with the player, e.g. a rival trying for the same promotion or the same love interest.
- They can **plot against** the player, **up to murder, with no guaranteed warning** (decision 10). Since the
  player's death ends the game, this makes the world truly dangerous:
  - **Plots follow the same rules as everyone else.** A killer needs a reason (an intent), a plan, the means
    (a weapon, poison, hired men), and the chance (finding the player alone).
  - **Signs may exist, but are not guaranteed.** Accomplices talk, rumours spread, enemies act strangely.
    A careful player who keeps friends, guards and good relations is safer.
  - **Plots can fail.** The player's skills, traits, equipment and companions can defeat an attempt.
- **Danger depends on the starting position (decision 13).** A common soldier has few enemies at first.
  If the player starts in a more important position, such as the mayor's secretary or the lord's bodyguard,
  they have rivals from the start: people who wanted that job, or who fear what they know.
- **A secret murder is very hard (decision 13).** The same rules apply to every character, including the player:
  - **Skill:** Intrigue to plan it and keep it secret, Martial for violence, Learning to make poison.
  - **Experience:** characters who have killed or schemed before do it better, e.g. Duelist, Schemer,
    or memories of past killings.
  - **Courage:** a Craven character will rarely try; a Brave or desperate one might.
  - **Weighing the penalty:** before any murder, the rules work out the chance of success and the penalty if it
    fails or is discovered, such as execution, exile, an infamy trait, or revenge from the victim's friends.
    The AI is given both. A sensible character only goes ahead if their intent is strong enough to be worth
    the risk. A rash, angry or desperate character may act anyway, and is more likely to fail.
  - **Most grudges therefore end in other ways:** rumours, sabotage, a duel, or a complaint to the lord.
    Murder is rare and serious.
- They can **help** the player too: a friend warns of an ambush; a grateful villager brings food.

### 5.7 Can the player see others' intents? (decision 12)
- Intents are **hidden by default**.
- The player learns them by **talking** (the character may tell a friend), **observing** what they do,
  **rumours**, **spying**, or through traits and skills, e.g. high Intrigue reveals hidden agendas.
- Once known, an intent is shown on the character's page, with how the player learned it.

### 5.8 Story characters and the plot (decision 9: fully free)
There are **no story locks**. Story characters pursue their intents freely, and any AI-played character may
change the plot: a rival may kill the mentor, the traitor may flee early, the lord's heir may die in a raid.
**The story must react to whatever happens.**

This means the plot is written as **situations and forces**, not a fixed script (proposal):
- **World events driven by the world, not by characters.** Some events happen on their own unless the world
  stops them, e.g. weather or a sickness. **But big story events come from characters, not from a script**
  (owner's decision, 2026-11-27): the enemy attack in Chapter 1 starts because the enemy general receives an
  order from his emperor, with a deadline of 1 month, and the player may persuade him not to attack, or surrender the town
  ([EVENTS.md §7](EVENTS.md)).
- **Story roles, not just story characters.** A beat that needs "someone opens the gate" is written for a
  role (*the traitor*). If the planned traitor is dead or exposed, the game looks for another character whose
  intents, traits and situation fit, e.g. a desperate Greedy merchant. If nobody fits, the beat does not happen
  and the story goes another way.
- **Branches for key characters.** For each important story character, the writer notes what happens if they
  die, flee or change sides early. The chapter's endings come from the state of the world at the end, not from a
  single fixed path.
- **Chapter goals stay fixed.** Chapter 1 is always about the invasion of the frontier town; how it plays out,
  and who lives, is open.
- **Cost of this freedom:** more writing (roles, branches, endings) and much more testing. The hidden
  `--autotest` mode can run many chapters quickly to find broken stories.

### 5.9 Example: the rival soldier
> **Edric**, a generated soldier in the player's unit, becomes AI-played when the player argues with him.
> - Traits: Ambitious, Deceitful, Brave. Background: a tanner's son who wants to escape his father's trade.
> - **Ambition:** become famous ("no one will call me tanner's boy again").
> - **Current aim:** be promoted to sergeant. The player is also a candidate.
> - **Plan (AI):** 1. Volunteer for the dangerous scouting patrol to impress the captain.
>   2. Tell the captain's friend that the player was drunk on watch (a lie, allowed by Deceitful).
>   3. Ask the captain for the promotion after the patrol.
> - **In real time:** the rules walk Edric to the captain, sign him up for the patrol, and later to the tavern
>   where he spreads the rumour. The player may notice the captain's opinion drop, hear the rumour from a friend,
>   or catch Edric in the act.
> - **If the patrol goes badly** (Edric is wounded), the plan fails, the AI re-plans, and his ambition may weaken
>   or turn into a grudge against whoever he blames.

## 6. Limiting the number of AI-played characters

Even with tiers, the active AI cast needs a cap to control cost and keep characters consistent.

| Rule (proposal) | Value |
|---|---|
| Story characters active per chapter | ~8–15 (set by the writer) |
| Encountered characters that can be AI-played at once | **~15–20** |
| When the cap is reached | The encountered character least relevant to the player (oldest, least contact) is demoted to rules |
| How often each AI-played character thinks | By triggers (§5.4), anywhere; at most about once per real minute |

**Total active AI cast: about 25–35 characters**, regardless of how big the world becomes.

## 7. The 10-minute time unit (decision 4)

The game runs in real time, with a basic unit of 10 real minutes. **What that unit equals in game time is
still open.** It strongly affects how long one life lasts:

| 10 real minutes = | Game time per real hour | A 40-year career takes about | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 game day | 6 days | ~2,400 hours | Far too long for a full life; fits a short chapter |
| 1 game week | 6 weeks | ~320 hours | Very long |
| 1 game month | 6 months | ~80 hours | Close to a typical long strategy game |
| 1 game season | 1.5 years | ~27 hours | Fast; a lifetime fits a few long sessions |

**Chapter 1 length (owner's decision, 2026-11-27):** Chapter 1 lasts about **1 month of game time**
([EVENTS.md §7.1](EVENTS.md)). For example, at 10 real minutes = 1 game day, that is about 5 real hours.

**Options to consider (TBD):**
- **Variable pace:** time slows during tense chapters (an invasion, a battle) and speeds up between them.
  For example, 10 minutes = 1 day during the invasion, and 1 month in peacetime.
- **Pause on events:** dialogue and decision events always pause the clock, so the player never misses them.
- **Speed control:** whether the player can fast-forward (e.g. 2× or 4×) or only pause.

For the AI, the time unit sets the pace of the **regular review** (§5.4). It also sets how much an AI character
can do between two of the player's actions.

## 8. Chapter 1 demo: AI cast and cost

**Setting:** a frontier town in one local county, facing an enemy invasion. The player starts as a soldier or
low officer.

### 8.1 Estimated cast

| Group | Examples | Count | Brain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Story characters | The county lord, the lord's heir or daughter, the player's captain, a mentor (old veteran), a rival soldier, a friend, the village priest or elder, a merchant or spy, the enemy commander, an enemy scout or traitor | **~8–12** | AI |
| Encountered characters | Soldiers in the player's unit, villagers the player helps, refugees, deserters | **up to ~15** (cap) | AI once met |
| Background characters | Other soldiers, villagers, enemy troops' officers | ~50–100 | Rules |

The demo limits the AI cast to **about 20–27 characters**.

### 8.2 Estimated AI calls per hour of play (updated for real-time play)

Real-time play with intents (§5) adds **planning calls**, and full AI everywhere (decision 11) means distant
characters plan as often as nearby ones. Estimate for ~25 AI-played characters:

| Call type | Per hour (estimate) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Planning and re-planning (§5.3–5.4) | ~520 | ~25 characters × about 3–4 per 10 real minutes, wherever they are |
| Important decisions | ~60 | Only when a decision is pending |
| AI-to-AI exchanges (§5.5) | ~60 | |
| Dialogue exchanges with the player | ~40 | e.g. ~10 conversations × 3–4 exchanges |
| Event text, profile and memory updates | ~30 | Optional parts included |
| **Total** | **~700** | Previously ~130, before real-time play |

### 8.3 Estimated cost

Assumptions per call:
- about 3,000 cached tokens (game rules plus the chapter's world and story description)
- about 1,500 uncached tokens (profile, memories, intents, knowledge, situation)
- about 300 output tokens (planning calls are a little longer than before)

| Model | Cost per call | **Per hour of play** | **Per demo playthrough (~2 hours)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claude Haiku 5.5 for everything | ≈ $0.0003 | ≈ $0.23 | **≈ $0.45** |
| Claude Sonnet 5.5 for everything | ≈ $0.0063 | ≈ $4.40 | **≈ $8.80** |
| Claude Opus 5.5 for everything | ≈ $0.013 | ≈ $8.80 | **≈ $18** |
| **Mix:** Sonnet 5.5 for dialogue with the player, Haiku 5.5 for planning and the rest | — | ≈ $0.47 | **≈ $0.95** |

- Thinking (the model reasoning before answering) can make outputs 1.5–3× longer. Use low effort for planning.
- **The mix is the recommended setup:** planning outputs are structured lists of actions, which the cheaper model handles
  well, while the player-facing dialogue gets the better writer.
- **Ways to lower cost further (proposal):** skip the regular review when nothing has changed, and let the rules
  handle obvious re-plans (e.g. the shop is closed, so come back tomorrow).
- **Development and testing** of the demo would likely cost a few tens of dollars in total. Testing the fully
  free plot with many `--autotest` runs adds to that; those runs can use the cheapest model.

**Conclusion:** real-time play everywhere makes AI use about **5× higher** than the earlier estimate. With the
mix, a full demo playthrough still costs **about a dollar** per player.

Prices are per million tokens (MTok). A token is about ¾ of an English word.

With the tier system, the full game's AI cast stays at about 25–35 characters. Costs therefore stay in the same
range as the demo: **roughly $0.25–$6 per hour of play** depending on the model, or about **$0.50 per hour with
the mix**. A 40-hour playthrough would cost about **$20 with the mix**.

For comparison, letting AI drive *every* character (500+) in real time would cost far more and would not be fast
enough. That option stays rejected.

## 11. Practical issues

- **Who pays:** either players bring their own API key, or the game calls through our own server
  (which needs a subscription or one-time "AI pack" pricing). **Never put an API key inside the game files.**
- **Offline fallback:** without AI, story and encountered characters fall back to rules and pre-written lines.
  Story characters therefore still need **basic hand-written lines** for key scenes, and **rule-based plans**
  for their intents. Because the plot is fully free, the rule-based fallback must also be able to pursue
  intents well enough for the story to keep moving.
- **Speed:** calls take 1–10 seconds.
  - Dialogue shows a short "thinking…" moment.
  - Planning runs in the background. The character keeps doing its current action, or something ordinary
    (working, eating), while it "thinks", so it never freezes.
- **Many calls at once:** with real-time play, several characters may need to think at the same moment.
  Calls are queued, and characters near the player go first.
- **Saves:** store all AI-written text, plans and decisions in the save file, so reloading doesn't change history.
- **Consistency:** send only true facts (from the character's own knowledge), and check every output against
  the rules.
- **Content:** war, murder plots, revenge and executions are normal medieval drama. Occasionally a request may
  be declined; fall back to rules.
- **Godot:** there is no official Anthropic SDK for GDScript. Use a small server written in Python or TypeScript
  with the official SDK, or plain HTTP requests from Godot's `HTTPRequest` node for the player-key option.
- **Store rules:** some stores (e.g. Steam) require disclosure of live AI-generated content.
